Who may benefit from EMO? 

National stakeholders — who and why

-	Central government ministries — finance, economy, interior, justice — need diagnostics to align policies, reduce contradictions, and improve crisis response.   

-	Central bank and financial regulators — monitor systemic risk, policy coherence, and market predictability to protect financial stability.   


-	Local and regional authorities — use coherence metrics to coordinate planning, reduce administrative friction, and improve service delivery.   

-	State‑owned enterprises and major utilities — benefit from clearer regulatory signals and more predictable procurement and investment environments.  


-	Large domestic corporations and industry associations — seek predictability for capital allocation, long‑term planning, and regulatory engagement.   

-	Institutional investors and pension funds — use systemic‑coherence indicators to assess country risk and stewardship priorities.   


-	Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) 

—	Gain from reduced legislative and administrative volatility that lowers compliance costs and uncertainty.  

-	Think tanks and policy research centres — adopt EMO outputs for evidence‑based recommendations and public debate.   

-	Universities and research institutes — partner on methodology, validation, and academic publications.  


-	Civil society organisations and consumer groups — monitor governance quality and hold institutions accountable.   

-	Media and business press — translate EMO findings into accessible narratives that influence public opinion and investor confidence.  



International stakeholders — who and why

-	Multilateral institutions (IMF, World Bank, EBRD) — integrate coherence diagnostics into governance assessments, conditionality, and technical assistance.   

-	OECD and regional policy bodies — compare regulatory quality and stakeholder engagement across countries; use EMO for benchmarking.   


-	Development finance institutions and bilateral donors — target reforms, measure impact, and reduce project risk through systemic coherence metrics.  

-	Sovereign and institutional investors (glotime-to-policy-alignment, an unds) — incorporate EMO into country‑risk models and stewardship strategies.   


-	Credit rating agencies — supplement sovereign assessments with structured measures of policy predictability and administrative friction.  

-	International consultancies and risk‑advisory firms — offer EMO‑based advisory services to clients entering or operating in a market.  


-	Global NGOs and advocacy networks — use EMO data to campaign for transparency, rule‑of‑law improvements, and anti‑corruption measures.  

-	Academic networks and research consortia — validate methodology, run cross‑country studies, and publish comparative analyses.  


-	Standards and stewardship bodies (ICGN, PRI, etc.) — integrate systemic coherence into stewardship toolkits and investor advocacy.

These groups combine decision‑making power, funding influence, and the ability to scale EMO’s impact.

 Ministries and central government agencies

Why it matters — Ministries need tools that reduce policy contradictions, improve crisis response, and make budgets more effective.  

Key benefits — faster crisis reaction, lower administrative waste, clearer cross‑ministry coordination.  

What EMO delivers — Regular coherence scorecards; policy correlation maps; early‑warning flags for conflicting regulations.  

Concrete metrics — Decision predictability index; policy alignment score; estimated fiscal leakage from incoherence.  

Engagement options — Pilot on a single ministry; quarterly dashboards; technical advisory to integrate EMO outputs into policy planning.  

Call to action — Request a 3‑month pilot scoped to one policy area (taxation, procurement, or infrastructure).


Central bank and financial regulators

Why it matters — Predictability and coordinated policy reduce systemic financial risk and improve market functioning.  

Key benefits — improved systemic risk signals, better stress‑test inputs, clearer macroprudential policy coordination.  

What EMO delivers — Coherence indicators feeding into risk models; scenario analysis for regulatory shocks.  

Concrete metrics — Policy shock propagation index, time-to-policy-alignment, and after crises.  

Engagement options — Integrate EMO outputs into supervisory dashboards; joint research on macro‑stability implications.  

Call to action — Propose a technical workshop with the macroprudential team to map use cases.

Concrete metrics — Permitting delay index; interagency contradiction count; projected cost savings per project.
